    qwen4_exp MTP: QSA rewind primitive + the PLE rewind design
    
    Step 4 groundwork. The two speculative carries need DIFFERENT rewind
    mechanisms, which is why one blanket "restore the snapshot" does not work:
    
    QSA — IMPLEMENTED. `QsaIndexer::rewind_seq_state(st, rows)`. `ingested` and
    `pooled` are contiguous marks, and raw keys / block keys are both written
    FORWARD from them, so a rewind is just moving the marks back; the bytes past
    them are stale but unreachable because the next ingest overwrites from the mark.
    `pooled` is recomputed rather than decremented, because a block that contained a
    rewound token must be re-pooled once its replacement arrives. No replay, no
    device copy.
    
    This matters because the ingest paths assert hard on position agreement
    (`pos == st.ingested`, `seq_start == st.ingested`). Without a rewind, a rejected
    draft leaves the carry one row ahead and the NEXT token trips that assert — the
    loud failure. The quiet one is a selection computed against keys that were never
    overwritten.
    
    PLE — needs a SNAPSHOT, not a mark rewind, and the design is recorded in
    verify_hc.rs. `PleSeqState::conv` is a rolling FP32 device convolution state and
    `history` is a fixed-length window whose oldest entries have already rolled off,
    so neither reconstructs by truncation. `snapshot_aux`/`restore_aux` already
    serialize both.
    
    ★ The placement is the subtle part, and it is the open decision for step 4:
    restoring a PRE-verify snapshot leaves the carries at `seq_len`, but a partial
    accept lands the sequence at `seq_len + accepted`. Snapshotting AFTER the
    committed row 0 and before the DRAFT rows makes restore land exactly right for
    the common gamma=1 case — accept keeps everything, reject restores to precisely
    "token_0 committed, draft discarded". That argues for verify running
    row-0-then-drafts rather than one K-row pass.
